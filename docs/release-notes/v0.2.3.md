LoopZ 0.2.3 — A lighter engine, more reliable audio setup

Route apps, microphones and audio interfaces into a virtual microphone for Zoom,
recording or streaming. This macOS update reduces background work and keeps your
workspace steady while you edit.

- **Quiet when idle.** Turning off every virtual device now stops audio rendering
  and capture. Control work sleeps between maintenance ticks and wakes for edits;
  unchanged status snapshots are reused. On the tested Apple Silicon Mac, idle
  Engine CPU with the window open fell from about 1.9% to 0.33% over 30 seconds.
- **Responsive controls.** Enabling a silent test device took 145–169 ms, including
  debug-client startup and status polling. That measures activation, not audio
  round-trip latency. Audio buffer sizes and DSP delays are unchanged.
- **Less unnecessary processing.** Physical-input conversion follows connected
  channels while meters continue to show every input. Capture and monitor checks
  reuse metadata between refreshes.
- **A stable workspace.** Routine apply progress stays in the footer. Alerts float
  over the workspace instead of moving the header, wires and channel sockets.
- **More reliable audio setup.** The native installer keeps its silent test device available until macOS sees it and the Engine acknowledges the configuration. Slow startup and changing device catalogs receive bounded checks with useful diagnostics.
- **A friendlier first run.** The whole-app tour uses a silent demo mix and explains
  sources, outputs, monitoring and Pass-through without changing your saved graph.

Install and repair audio components in LoopZ Settings. First-time driver activation may ask you to choose **Finish audio setup**; sound briefly pauses while macOS loads the driver. Ordinary updates keep the audio service running.

This release is for macOS 14.2 or later. Windows remains a separate experimental
preview. Small-buffer performance depends on your hardware and system load;
one hardware buffer is not total end-to-end latency.
